The Lab

Over the semester I completed four robotics labs. The last one was the hardest. My partner and I had to program the robot to escape a maze it had never seen. It had no map. It had to find the walls on its own, choose its path as it went and reach the exit without crashing.

How It Works

We built the maze as objects in Python. A Cell class stores each square's position, its open neighbors, whether it has been visited and its cost. A Maze class holds every cell and decides where to go next. Each cell's cost is how many steps it is from the exit.

After every move the robot runs the same loop. It reads its left, front and right infrared sensors to find walls. It removes any blocked paths from its map. A flood fill step recomputes the costs. Then it turns toward the cheapest neighbor it has not visited yet and drives one cell. For safety, a bump or a button press stops the robot and turns its light solid red. When it reaches the exit, it stops and spins a green light.

What I Learned

The big lesson was to break a hard problem into small pieces. We wrote and tested each helper function on its own before putting it on the robot. That made bugs much easier to find. I also saw that the shortest route is not always the one you take first. The robot only knows what it has sensed so far, so it has to adapt as it learns.
